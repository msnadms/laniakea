# Colonization campaign

The six phases in `colonization-and-kardashev-plan.md` are connected through the existing production and logistics systems.

## Playing the campaign

1. Upgrade a habitable world's fabricator to tier 2. Choose **Stage colony charter** in its planet panel.
2. Connect a route to the new colony hexagon. Deliver one of each settlement assembly, including an Ectogenesis Bank. Rares already in the stockpile use incoming route bandwidth; freshly manufactured rares can travel directly along the route. The charter buffer persists between dispatches.
3. Bring the Peregrine to that planet's system and charter it. The settlement assemblies abstract everything needed to establish its initial 100 people. Deliver nutrients and supply local industry.
4. Open **Humanity** beside the HUD's alien-matter count, or select the colony on the logistics map. Install delivered assemblies, place standing orders for more, read the colony's food runway, commission construction, and evacuate from these controls.
5. At Type I, commission a Dyson swarm. Deliver 60 Statite Mirrors, 60 Thermophotovoltaic Films and 30 Momentum Tethers, and provide 10,000 population-hours of labor. No stellar output is awarded before completion.
6. At a completed swarm, commission a replication network: 30 Self-Replicating Probes and 5,000 labor. Three completed networks at different stars in one galaxy represent galactic saturation and unlock Type III.

## Initial balance and decisions

- Charter assemblies are the complete founding cost; biological seed stock is intentionally abstracted into the Ectogenesis Bank. Ecology columns provide 1,000 capacity each. All extractors and fabricators in a colony’s system operate locally while the Peregrine is away; nutrient extractors receive priority.
- Extractor code currently produces its displayed rate per **real second**, despite existing hourly labels. Consumption is therefore 18 nutrients per person per real hour: a mature 1,000-person colony consumes 5/second, roughly one good unmodified nutrient extractor. Colony food buffers hold two hours at capacity, and a colony advertises only a fifteen-minute delivery window at a time, so it ramps up alongside the fabricators sharing its edges instead of emptying them in one dispatch. It requests no settlement assembly it has no standing order for, and exports everything else it builds. Extraction buffers and reserve settings still apply when elapsed time is processed; this is client-side automation, not a server running while the app is closed.
- Growth stops without food. After a 30-minute grace period, starvation removes 100 people/hour continuously; the UI reports whole people and the colony's remaining food runway. A colony with zero people cannot regrow. Because nothing simulates a closed tab, an unattended gap is simulated only as far as a colony can stock for — two hours — and yields at most four escapes, so an absence costs stores and attention rather than the settlement. Labor uses the exact integrated fed population. Population, buffers, clocks and cumulative losses persist.
- Type I requires 500 people and one continuous hour of local food surplus, with no external nutrient deliveries during the preceding hour; a colony whose food store is already full advertises no nutrient demand, so routes stop delivering on their own and the clock can run. Ammunition and construction deliveries do not count as food dependence. Type II and III impose permanent attention floors of one and two bars. Tier achievements remain after losing infrastructure.
- Probes escape at two attention bars when no shot is available, at most once per 30 seconds per defended location — or above a floor plus one, so a Kardashev attention floor never leaks exposure by itself. At four bars more probes converge than one mount can engage, and one escapes each interval even while the gun is firing. Exposure never decays. Only a kill made at observation range — two bars or above — leaves recoverable wreckage, and that wreckage advertises itself, so such a kill suppresses half a bar rather than a whole one. Holding attention high enough to salvage is therefore a deliberate, expensive gamble rather than free income. No planet, extractor, recipe or debug cargo refill generates alien matter.
- Sentinel Ammunition costs three helium-3 per round, just like ship ammunition. Batteries store 40 rounds each; five rounds destroy a probe. Additional batteries shorten the 30-second interval. Each local industry raises attention by four bars an hour against a decay of two, so defense costs roughly 15 helium-3 an hour for one industry and 450 for eight — a gradient rather than a threshold. Route arrivals add a hundredth of a bar each.
- Antihydrogen reservoirs store 2,000 of each warp fuel per installation. A funded, living colony on a route pays its dispatch cost before the ship is considered. Local factory output stays at the colony and can leave through outgoing routes, sharing the same bandwidth limits as other materials.
- The first strike is telegraphed at exposure 20. Later strikes require another 100 exposure after their warning. Each targets a named supercluster with ten minutes to evacuate. An evacuation preserves the people in a separate flotilla count; installed colony assemblies and construction are abandoned. Flotillas are counted as survivors, not as productive colonies. The original tier-2 fabricator remains available for a new charter.
- The existing Peregrine death/reset behavior remains the campaign loss condition, including after colonization. The campaign has no endings yet: Type III is the current ceiling and play continues past it. Type IV is lore, not a player unlock.

## Persistence and verification

Colonies have their own Firestore collection. `persistFabricatorRun` accepts colony keys and includes campaign accounting and stockpile writes. Its per-user write queue prevents an older colony save overtaking a later evacuation or strike deletion. Colony UI mutations subscribe to the same fan-out, and automation continues polling after a persistence error. Existing rare IDs, including `frame_dragging_gyro`, are unchanged.

Regression coverage includes logistic growth and starvation, the clamped unattended catch-up and its escape ceiling, founding accounting and ship presence, probe cooldowns and saturation, wreckage gated on observation range, the industry attention gradient, delivery windows and standing orders, alien recipe inputs, shared nutrient demand, rare filtering and bandwidth, route sponsorship, remote local industry, megastructure completion, ladder boundaries, strike evacuation/destruction, and persistence ordering. Firestore writes follow the repository's existing multi-document save pattern; they are not a cross-client transaction.
